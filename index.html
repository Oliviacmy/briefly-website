Hong Kong News Friday · 16 May 2026

Health authorities halt use of medical devices after patient death linked to catheter abnormality

Hong Kong health authorities ordered a halt to the use of medical devices tied to the death of an elderly patient after a heart operation at Tuen Mun Hospital. A preliminary inspection found an abnormality in the screw-on connector at the end of a catheter used during the procedure. The incident adds to scrutiny of hospital safety, device oversight and how quickly authorities respond when medical equipment is suspected in a fatal case.

Source: SCMP

香港衞生當局下令停用一批與屯門醫院一名長者心臟手術後死亡有關的醫療儀器。初步檢查發現,手術期間使用的導管末端螺絲接駁位出現異常。事件令外界進一步關注醫院安全、儀器監管,以及當局在涉及致命懷疑醫療事故時的應對速度。

Why it matters

Device-related deaths immediately raise questions about how often equipment is inspected, how clearly incidents are reported, and how quickly hospitals act when something fails. For Hong Kong readers, it also tests confidence in public-hospital governance.

Global News Friday · 16 May 2026

Hormuz pressure builds as Trump says Xi agrees strait must reopen, but China stays cautious

Pressure on the Strait of Hormuz intensified after Donald Trump said Xi Jinping agreed the waterway must reopen to shipping, while Beijing’s public response remained measured. The strait carries roughly a fifth of the world’s oil, and any sustained disruption flows quickly into freight rates, insurance premiums and inflation across Asia.

Source: Reuters · AP

霍爾木茲海峽再現壓力。特朗普表示,習近平同意該海峽必須重新開放予船運,但北京的官方回應仍然審慎。該海峽承載全球約五分之一原油運輸,任何持續中斷,都會迅速反映在亞洲的運費、保險費及通脹之上。

Why it matters

Disruption in Hormuz can affect oil prices, shipping costs and inflation across Asia. For Hong Kong readers, the conflict’s impact is likely to appear through markets and prices before it appears directly in daily life.
